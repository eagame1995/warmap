Pre-Intermediate • Video lesson

What’s Good for You?

Healthy eating, the story of chocolate, and cheesemaking. Prediction, while-watching, post-watching, and speaking practice.
Lead-in Part 1: You are what you eat Part 2: The chocolate story Part 3: Cheesemaking
Lead-in

Food, health & habits

Discuss the questions. Give reasons and examples. Try to speak for 30–45 seconds for each question.

1. What does a “healthy diet” mean to you?
2. Can food be healthy if it contains fat or sugar? Why / why not?
3. Why do people often choose fast food even when they know it is not very healthy?
4. Do you think people worry too much about “healthy” and “unhealthy” food?
5. Which is more important: what you eat or how much you eat?
6. What food do you think people will eat more of in 20 years?
7. Can chocolate be part of a healthy diet? Explain your opinion.
8. Why do traditional foods stay popular even when new food trends appear?

Task 2. Before you watch: agree or disagree

Choose your opinion first. After watching, check whether the video supports or challenges it.

1. Fat is always bad for you.
2. Cheese can have health benefits.
3. Dark chocolate may be healthier than milk chocolate.
4. Experts never change their advice about food.
5. Busy people usually have more time to cook healthy food.
6. Sugar is the only unhealthy thing in our diet.
7. Fish is usually presented as a healthy choice.

Task 4. Complete the sentences

Post-watching

Task 5. Match the idea to the explanation

Match each idea with the best explanation.

Task 6. Think deeper

Answer in 1–3 sentences.

1. Why is it difficult for people to eat healthily today?
2. Why can advice about food change over time?

Speaking challenge — Create your own chocolate bar

Invent a new chocolate bar. Speak for 45–60 seconds. Include: type of chocolate, 2–3 extra ingredients, shape, target customer, and one reason people would buy it.

Useful language: It would contain… / I’d add… / It would be shaped like… / It would be for… / People would buy it because…
